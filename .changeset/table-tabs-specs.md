Two more component specs, both drafts: `table` and `tabs`, each with its page in the style guide. A table is the prose table's: a frame with the large radius that it scrolls in, its head on the surface as an overline, rows apart by lines and amounts at the end of their column in figures of one width. A table whose rows can be chosen raises a row under the pointer, gives a chosen row the selection color, rings a focused row inside it, and keeps the chosen row in the selection colors in forced colors. Tabs are the frame's tabs as views switched in place, in Nuxt UI's five sizes: a row of mono labels over a line, the current tab marked by a bar of the brand color, which keeps the selection color in forced colors. Nuxt UI's pill tabs have no spec: the segmented control covers that use. Their implementations record what ui-vue draws today and where it does not meet them yet.

The component tier gains the `table` group, which the prose table reads too: the head `--ws-table-head-bg` and `--ws-table-head-text`, the room in each cell `--ws-table-cell-padding-inline` and `--ws-table-cell-padding-block`, a row's text `--ws-table-row-text`, and its fill under the pointer and once chosen, `--ws-table-row-bg-hover` and `--ws-table-row-bg-selected`. The prose table looks as it did. The tabs have no group: each of their states is a role that already names its use.

A spec row can set `text-align` and `font-variant-numeric`. In forced colors the `testing` helper compares the pixels each part covers at least half of, so a row at a fraction of a pixel no longer takes in the line under it, and it leaves out a part drawn at another size in the two examples, such as the bar under another tab, where it used to find a difference that did not show a state.
